Maintenance & Salvage Therapy
Framework
Cross-tumor principle
Maintenance examples
Bevacizumab or Capecitabine maintenance post-FOLFOX (CRC); Rucaparib/Olaparib maintenance post-platinum (ovarian, BRCA-mutated); PD-1 maintenance post-chemo-immunotherapy (NSCLC)
Salvage principle
Regimen selection guided by prior therapy exposure, resistance mechanism, biomarker re-testing at progression, and performance status
maintenance therapy salvage therapy parp inhibitor olaparib rucaparib bevacizumab progression relapse
Operational Rules
Dose Modification Rules
Rule Set
  • ANC < 1000/µL or platelets < 75,000/µL on day of treatment → delay cycle
  • Grade ≥3 non-hematologic toxicity → hold until resolution to ≤Grade 1, resume at reduced dose (typically 75–80%)
  • Renal/hepatic impairment-based dose capping (agent-specific creatinine clearance / bilirubin thresholds)
  • Cumulative dose ceilings (e.g., doxorubicin 450–550 mg/m² lifetime, bleomycin 400 units lifetime)
dose modification rules neutropenia thrombocytopenia dose reduction delay cumulative dose
Standard Premedications
Rule Set
  • Taxanes: Dexamethasone + H1 blocker (diphenhydramine) + H2 blocker (ranitidine/famotidine)
  • Platinum agents: 5-HT3 antagonist + NK1 antagonist + dexamethasone (highly emetogenic tier)
  • Rituximab / mAbs: Paracetamol + antihistamine ± hydrocortisone before first infusions
  • Cisplatin: pre- and post-hydration with electrolyte supplementation, mannitol/furosemide if indicated
premedication antiemetic prophylaxis hypersensitivity taxane platinum rituximab
Growth Factor Support
Rule Set
  • Primary G-CSF prophylaxis when febrile neutropenia risk of regimen ≥20% (e.g., TPF, dose-dense AC)
  • Secondary prophylaxis after a prior febrile neutropenia episode without dose reduction option
  • Filgrastim daily until ANC recovery, or single dose pegfilgrastim 24h post-chemotherapy
  • ESA consideration for chemotherapy-induced anemia per Hb threshold and guideline criteria
g-csf growth factor filgrastim pegfilgrastim febrile neutropenia prophylaxis esa anemia
Drug Interaction Flags
Rule Set
  • CYP3A4 inhibitors/inducers with vinca alkaloids, taxanes, TKIs — dose/level adjustment
  • Warfarin + capecitabine/5-FU → enhanced anticoagulant effect, monitor INR closely
  • Live vaccines contraindicated during active immunosuppressive chemotherapy
  • QT-prolonging co-medications with agents such as osimertinib, vandetanib, arsenic trioxide

Organ-at-Risk (OAR) Constraints
Spinal Cord
OAR
Constraint type: Maximum point dose — conventional fractionation ceiling typically ≤45–50 Gy depending on cord length irradiated; stricter limits for re-irradiation and SBRT.

Parotid
OAR
Constraint type: Mean dose — spare at least one parotid to reduce xerostomia; contralateral sparing prioritized in unilateral disease.

OAR
Constraint type: Mean dose — bilateral mean dose limitation critical in abdominal/paraaortic fields to preserve renal function.

OAR
Constraint type: V30 volumetric — percentage of liver volume receiving ≥30 Gy kept below threshold to avoid radiation-induced liver disease.

OAR

Contouring Atlas & Planning Protocols
Volume Definitions
Atlas
  • GTV — Gross Tumor Volume, visible/palpable disease
  • CTV — Clinical Target Volume, GTV + subclinical microscopic spread margin
  • ITV — Internal Target Volume, CTV + physiological motion margin
  • PTV — Planning Target Volume, ITV/CTV + setup uncertainty margin
  • OAR — Organ at Risk, structures whose tolerance dose constrains the plan

Workflow
  • Immobilization device selection (thermoplastic mask, vac-bag, wing-board)
  • CT simulation slice thickness and contrast protocol per site
  • Bolus usage for skin/scar dose build-up
  • Image guidance frequency (daily CBCT vs weekly kV pairs)
  • Adaptive RT trigger criteria (anatomical change, weight loss, tumor regression)

Breast Lump Work-up
Algorithm
  • Clinical exam → Diagnostic mammography + targeted ultrasound
  • BI-RADS categorization → core needle biopsy if BI-RADS 4/5
  • Receptor panel: ER, PR, HER2 on biopsy
  • Staging: CT chest/abdomen/pelvis or PET-CT, bone scan if symptomatic/high-risk, MRI breast for extent
  • Tumor marker: CA 15-3 / CEA for baseline & monitoring (not diagnostic)

Algorithm
  • CT chest characterization → PET-CT for FDG-avidity/staging

  • Brain MRI for staging in non-squamous / advanced disease
  • Reflex molecular testing on biopsy: EGFR, ALK, ROS1, PD-L1 (see Molecular Master)

Invasion Assessment
Reference
Lymphovascular invasion (LVI): tumor cells within endothelium-lined vascular/lymphatic spaces — adverse prognostic factor across most solid tumors.

Perineural invasion (PNI): tumor cells tracking along/around nerve sheaths — associated with local recurrence risk, particularly in prostate, pancreatic and head & neck cancers.

Cancer Staging Master
Structured AJCC/UICC staging engine — TNM definitions, stage grouping logic, prognostic staging and site-specific rules.
TNM Framework
Core Logic
  • T — primary Tumor extent/size (T0–T4, subcategories a/b)
  • N — regional lymph Node involvement (N0–N3)
  • M — distant Metastasis (M0 / M1)
  • Prefix modifiers: c (clinical), p (pathologic), yc/yp (post-neoadjuvant), r (recurrent)
tnm staging t stage n stage m stage ajcc uicc clinical pathologic
Stage Grouping
Core Logic
TNM combinations are mapped to Stage 0 through Stage IV per site-specific AJCC rules — e.g. Breast Stage IIA = T0-1N1M0 or T2N0M0; Colon Stage III = any T, N1-2, M0; Lung Stage IV = any T, any N, M1. Each edition (currently AJCC 8th) refines cut-points using outcomes data.
stage grouping stage 0 i ii iii iv ajcc 8th edition breast colon lung
Prognostic Staging
Refinement
Certain sites (notably breast cancer) layer biomarker data — grade, ER/PR/HER2 status, genomic recurrence score — onto anatomic TNM to produce a Prognostic Stage Group that better reflects outcome than anatomic stage alone.
prognostic staging biomarker grade er pr her2 oncotype recurrence score
Site-Specific Rules
Examples
  • Breast: Tis includes DCIS/LCIS/Paget disease without invasive component
  • Colon: T4b defined by direct invasion of adjacent organs/structures
  • Cervix: FIGO staging integrated alongside TNM, incorporates imaging/pathologic findings per 2018 revision
  • Lung: T-category driven by size thresholds (≤1, ≤2, ≤3, ≤4, ≤5, ≤7, >7 cm) and invasion features

Toxicity Management Master
CTCAE-based grading with recommended management, dose modification and rechallenge guidance for major treatment-related toxicities.
Febrile Neutropenia
CTCAE
Definition: ANC <500/µL (or <1000 with predicted decline) + single temperature ≥38.3°C (or ≥38.0°C sustained 1h).
Management: Empiric broad-spectrum IV antibiotics within 1 hour, risk stratification (MASCC score), blood cultures before antibiotics.
Dose modification: Reduce subsequent cycle dose or add G-CSF prophylaxis.
Rechallenge: Permitted with growth factor support unless recurrent despite prophylaxis.

Mucositis
CTCAE
Grades: 1 (asymptomatic/mild) → 4 (life-threatening, urgent intervention).
Management: Oral hygiene protocols, topical analgesics, cryotherapy during bolus 5-FU, opioid analgesia for severe grades, nutritional support.
Dose modification: Hold chemotherapy/RT for Grade ≥3 until resolution to ≤Grade 1.

Diarrhea
CTCAE
Grades: 1 (<4 stools/day above baseline) → 4 (life-threatening).
Management: Loperamide for uncomplicated Grade 1–2; hospitalization, IV fluids, octreotide consideration for Grade ≥3 or complicated features.
Dose modification: Hold irinotecan/5-FU-based regimens until resolved; reduce dose on rechallenge.
diarrhea ctcae grading management loperamide octreotide irinotecan
Pneumonitis
CTCAE
Grades: 1 (radiographic only) → 4 (life-threatening respiratory compromise).
Management: Corticosteroids for Grade ≥2, hold offending agent (immunotherapy/targeted therapy/RT), pulmonology input, consider bronchoscopy to exclude infection.
Rechallenge: Cautious rechallenge for Grade 1–2 after resolution; generally avoided for Grade ≥3.

Hepatitis (Immune/Drug-related)
CTCAE
Grades: based on ALT/AST and bilirubin elevation multiples of ULN.
Management: Hold therapy, corticosteroids for Grade ≥2, mycophenolate for steroid-refractory immune-related hepatitis.
Rechallenge: Individualized; avoid in Grade 4 or steroid-refractory cases.
hepatitis ctcae grading management alt ast corticosteroids immune related
Cardiotoxicity
CTCAE
Grades: based on LVEF decline and symptomatic heart failure severity.
Management: Cardiology referral, ACE inhibitor/beta-blocker initiation, hold anthracycline/anti-HER2 therapy for significant LVEF drop.
Monitoring: Serial echo/MUGA at defined intervals during and after cardiotoxic regimens.
cardiotoxicity ctcae grading lvef management anthracycline her2 heart failure
Peripheral Neuropathy
CTCAE
Grades: 1 (asymptomatic) → 3 (limiting self-care ADL); Grade 4 rare.
Management: Dose reduction or discontinuation of offending taxane/platinum/vinca agent at Grade ≥2 with functional impairment; duloxetine for established painful neuropathy.
Prevention: Cold avoidance for oxaliplatin, cumulative dose tracking.
peripheral neuropathy ctcae grading management taxane platinum vinca duloxetine
Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS)
CTCAE
Grades: 1 (fever only) → 4 (life-threatening, multi-organ, ventilator/pressor requirement) — commonly graded by ASTCT consensus.
Management: Supportive care for Grade 1; Tocilizumab (IL-6 receptor blockade) ± corticosteroids for Grade ≥2; ICU-level support for severe grades.
Context: Principally seen with CAR-T cell therapy and bispecific antibody constructs.

Risk-Stratified

  • Moderate risk (AC, oxaliplatin): 5-HT3 antagonist + dexamethasone
  • Low risk: single agent 5-HT3 antagonist or dexamethasone

Pain Management
WHO Ladder
  • Step 1 — Non-opioid (paracetamol, NSAID) ± adjuvant
  • Step 2 — Weak opioid (tramadol, codeine) for mild-moderate pain
  • Step 3 — Strong opioid (morphine, oxycodone, fentanyl) for moderate-severe pain
  • Adjuvants: gabapentinoids for neuropathic pain, bisphosphonates/denosumab for bone pain, corticosteroids for compressive pain

The Clinical Knowledge Graph

Rather than functioning as fourteen isolated databases, this reference is designed to operate as an interconnected Clinical Knowledge Graph. The Disease Master sits as the central hub, linked outward to Staging, Pathology, Biomarkers, Surgery, Radiation, Systemic Therapy, Toxicity Management and Follow-up. For a given patient, the graph can be traversed to answer:

  • What is the correct stage?
  • Is surgery indicated?
  • Which chemotherapy regimens are preferred?
  • What radiation dose and fractionation are recommended?
  • Which biomarkers should be tested?
  • What toxicities should be monitored?
  • What is the recommended follow-up schedule?
